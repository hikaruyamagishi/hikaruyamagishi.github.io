I am a political economist who studies how institutions shape representation and economic outcomes. Since 2024, I have been part of the Women, Business and the Law project at the World Bank in Washington, D.C., researching how laws and institutions shape women's economic opportunities. I apply a mix of research methods, combining qualitative and quantitative analyses. My work on political representation, democracy, and gender has been published in top peer-reviewed journals (e.g., _American Political Science Review_, _American Journal of Political Science_). 

I hold a B.A. from Dartmouth College, an M.A. from Yale University, and a Ph.D. in Political Science from Yale University. Before pursuing a Ph.D. at Yale University, I worked on a fixed-income trading floor (interest rates and derivatives) at a Japanese investment bank in New York City, covering global central banks and Japanese institutional investors.
